Tomato: Water tomato plants deeply at the base in the morning. Keep soil evenly moist, avoid wetting leaves, and add mulch to reduce evaporation. Support tall plants with stakes and remove damaged leaves. Check regularly for early blight; rotate crops and avoid overhead irrigation.
Tomato: For yellowing tomato leaves, first check soil moisture and drainage. Water only when the top layer begins to dry, and ensure containers have drainage holes. Remove badly affected leaves and inspect for pests before applying any treatment.
Tomato: To reduce fruit cracking, maintain consistent soil moisture. Use mulch around the plant, avoid sudden heavy watering after a dry period, and harvest ripe fruits promptly.
Tomato: If aphids appear on tomato plants, inspect the undersides of leaves. A firm water spray can dislodge small colonies. Encourage beneficial insects and avoid broad-spectrum pesticides that may harm them.
Rice: For rice seedlings, prepare a level nursery bed and use healthy, certified seed. Keep the nursery moist but not deeply flooded during establishment. Transplant vigorous seedlings at the locally recommended age and spacing.
Rice: Monitor rice fields regularly for stem borer damage. Look for dead hearts in vegetative growth and whiteheads near maturity. Remove heavily affected plant residues and consult local agricultural guidance before choosing a control method.
Rice: Manage water carefully in rice fields. Maintain a shallow water layer when appropriate, and drain fields when recommended for the crop stage and local soil conditions. Avoid wasting irrigation water.
Rice: For weeds in rice, use clean planting material and timely field preparation. Hand weed or use locally approved integrated weed management methods at the correct crop stage. Follow product labels and local extension advice.
Wheat: Sow wheat in a well-prepared, well-drained field at the locally recommended time. Use certified seed and a variety suited to the region. Apply nutrients based on a soil test rather than guesswork.
Wheat: If wheat leaves show yellow or orange powdery spots, inspect for rust symptoms. Remove volunteer wheat where practical and seek local extension confirmation. Use resistant varieties and only locally recommended fungicides when needed.
Wheat: Irrigate wheat at critical growth stages according to soil moisture and local recommendations. Avoid waterlogging, especially in heavy soils, and check field conditions before each irrigation.
Wheat: For aphids in wheat, inspect leaf sheaths and ears. Conserve ladybird beetles and other natural enemies. Avoid spraying when populations are low; seek local threshold-based advice.
Maize: Plant maize in moist, well-drained soil using healthy seed and locally recommended spacing. Keep the field free of early weed competition and apply fertilizer based on soil testing.
Maize: For fall armyworm, scout maize whorls regularly for window-pane feeding and fresh frass. Use integrated pest management, destroy heavily infested plant parts when practical, and contact local extension services for approved options.
Maize: If maize leaves curl, check for water stress, aphids, and herbicide injury. Examine several plants across the field before deciding on action. Correct irrigation issues and seek diagnosis if symptoms spread.
Maize: Apply mulch or retain suitable crop residue to conserve soil moisture. Rotate crops where possible and avoid repeated maize cultivation without soil-health practices.
Potato: Plant certified, disease-free potato seed tubers in loose, well-drained soil. Earth up around plants as they grow to protect developing tubers from sunlight. Avoid waterlogging.
Potato: If potato leaves develop dark lesions with pale edges, isolate the affected area and seek diagnosis for possible late blight. Avoid overhead watering, remove infected material safely, and follow local disease alerts.
Potato: Maintain even soil moisture during tuber development. Irregular watering can affect tuber quality. Use clean seed and rotate away from potatoes and other related crops.
Onion: Grow onion in a sunny bed with loose, well-drained soil. Water consistently while plants establish, then reduce irrigation as bulbs mature. Avoid excess nitrogen late in the season.
Onion: For thrips, inspect young leaves and the inner leaf folds. Reduce nearby weed hosts and use locally approved integrated pest management. Recheck plants after any intervention.
Chilli: Provide chilli plants with well-drained soil and steady moisture. Avoid waterlogging and use mulch to reduce weeds. Inspect leaves regularly for mites, aphids, and virus symptoms.
Chilli: If chilli leaves curl, inspect for whiteflies, aphids, and mites. Remove severely affected plants when a virus is suspected and control weed hosts. Get local diagnosis before applying pesticides.
Groundnut: Sow groundnut in warm, well-drained soil. Use healthy seed and follow local spacing guidance. Avoid excessive irrigation and keep the field free of weeds during early growth.
Groundnut: Rotate groundnut with cereals to help break pest and disease cycles. Inspect leaves for leaf spots and avoid working in wet fields when disease spread is likely.
Cotton: Scout cotton fields weekly for sucking pests and bollworm signs. Record pest levels, conserve beneficial insects, and use integrated pest management thresholds recommended by local extension officers.
Cotton: For water stress in cotton, check soil moisture below the surface and inspect plants across the field. Irrigate according to crop stage and local water availability; avoid prolonged waterlogging.
Soybean: Plant soybean in well-drained soil with quality seed and locally recommended spacing. Inoculate seed only with a compatible, approved rhizobium product when advised. Rotate crops and monitor weeds early.
Soybean: If soybean leaves show spots, note the pattern and whether symptoms occur on older or younger leaves. Improve field airflow, avoid unnecessary leaf wetness, and seek local diagnosis before treatment.
Sugarcane: Use healthy, disease-free setts and varieties recommended for the region. Maintain suitable row spacing, irrigate according to soil moisture, and keep young fields free from weeds.
Sugarcane: Earthing up supports sugarcane stalks and can help manage weeds. Time field operations according to crop growth and soil moisture, and avoid damaging roots.
Banana: Keep banana soil consistently moist but not waterlogged. Apply organic mulch, remove dry leaves, and provide support to fruiting plants where needed. Use clean planting material.
Banana: For suspected Panama disease, do not move soil or planting material from affected areas. Clean tools between plants and contact local plant-health authorities for confirmation and guidance.
Mango: Water young mango trees during dry periods while allowing the root zone to drain. Mulch around the tree but keep mulch away from the trunk. Prune dead or diseased branches with clean tools.
Mango: Monitor mango flowers and young fruit for pests and disease. Remove fallen diseased material and seek local advice before spraying during flowering to protect pollinators.
Papaya: Plant papaya in a sunny, well-drained location. Avoid standing water around roots and use clean seedlings. Inspect plants for mosaic patterns and abnormal leaf distortion.
Papaya: If papaya ringspot is suspected, isolate affected plants and seek local confirmation. Use healthy seedlings, manage nearby weed hosts, and follow regional disease-management guidance.
Coconut: Maintain a clean basin around young coconut palms and mulch to conserve moisture. Avoid piling soil against the trunk and apply nutrients based on local recommendations and soil condition.
Coconut: Inspect coconut leaves and crown regularly for unusual damage. Remove only safely accessible dead material and contact trained local services for pest or disease diagnosis.
Okra: Sow okra in warm soil with adequate spacing and sunlight. Water at the base when the soil begins to dry and harvest pods regularly while tender.
Okra: Check okra leaves for yellow vein mosaic symptoms and whiteflies. Remove severely affected plants, manage weeds, and use resistant varieties where available.
Cabbage: Grow cabbage in fertile, well-drained soil and keep moisture steady during head formation. Rotate with unrelated crops and inspect leaf undersides for caterpillar eggs.
Cabbage: For caterpillars, handpick eggs and larvae in small plots and encourage natural enemies. Use insect netting where practical and follow local integrated pest management advice.
Cauliflower: Keep cauliflower evenly watered during curd formation. Avoid large moisture fluctuations and provide suitable soil fertility based on testing. Remove crop residues after harvest.
Brinjal: Inspect brinjal shoots and fruits frequently for shoot and fruit borer damage. Remove and destroy affected shoots and fruits, use pheromone traps where locally recommended, and rotate crops.
Brinjal: Water brinjal at the base and avoid waterlogging. Mulch to conserve moisture and inspect leaves for mites, aphids, and whiteflies.
Lentil: Sow lentil in well-drained soil at the locally recommended time. Avoid excessive nitrogen and monitor soil moisture, especially during flowering and pod formation.
Chickpea: Use clean seed and a locally suitable variety. Avoid sowing into waterlogged soil and scout for pod borer eggs and larvae. Follow local threshold-based integrated pest management.
Mustard: Ensure good drainage in mustard fields and use recommended seed rates. Scout for aphids on flowering shoots and conserve natural enemies before considering control measures.
Coffee: Maintain shade and ground cover appropriate to the coffee system. Prune for airflow, remove diseased berries, and monitor for coffee berry borer with local extension guidance.
Tea: Keep tea fields well-drained and maintain suitable shade and soil cover. Prune according to local recommendations and monitor young leaves for mites and other pests.
Turmeric: Plant healthy turmeric rhizomes in loose, well-drained soil. Mulch after planting, maintain moisture without waterlogging, and rotate crops to reduce disease pressure.
Ginger: Use healthy seed rhizomes and raised beds where drainage is poor. Avoid standing water, remove diseased plants carefully, and clean tools before moving between plots.
General: Before applying fertilizer, test the soil where possible and follow region-specific nutrient recommendations. Apply the right source, amount, time, and placement to reduce waste and protect water.
General: Conserve water by checking soil moisture before irrigation, repairing leaks, and using drip or other efficient methods where suitable. Match irrigation to crop stage, soil type, and local weather.
General: Rotate crops with different plant families and include legumes where suitable. Rotation can help interrupt pest and disease cycles and support soil fertility.
General: Use integrated pest management: monitor fields, identify the pest correctly, set action thresholds, conserve beneficial organisms, and choose the least disruptive effective control.
General: Remove diseased crop residues when advised, clean tools, use healthy planting material, and avoid moving infested soil between fields.
General: Add well-decomposed organic matter to improve soil structure. Avoid using fresh manure close to harvest or planting unless local guidance confirms it is safe and suitable.
General: Keep field records of planting dates, irrigation, fertilizer, pest observations, and treatments. Records help compare seasons and make better decisions.
General: Always identify the crop, growth stage, symptoms, and field conditions before choosing a treatment. Recommendations vary by region, soil, climate, and local regulations.
